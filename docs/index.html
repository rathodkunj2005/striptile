Every window gets a place in the row.

StripTile lines your windows up side by side and lets you slide along them. Nothing hides behind anything else, and nothing is resized behind your back.

Download StripTile View the source

Free and open source under the MIT license. Requires macOS 13 or later on Apple silicon.

StripTile

Drag the row, scroll sideways, or press the arrow keys. This is a small imitation of the real thing.

What it does

One row per display
A new window opens next to the one you are using. Windows already in the row keep their size.
Four-finger swipe
Slide along the row with four fingers. It keeps coasting after you let go, then settles on the nearest column.
Window picker
Press ⌃⌥Space, type a few letters of an app or a title, and jump to that window.
Row overview
See every column to scale at once. Pick one with the arrow keys or a click.
Rules for each app
Choose whether an app tiles, floats or is ignored, and the width its windows open at.
A pinned side window
Pin one window to the left or right edge. The row scrolls beside it instead of underneath it.
Stacks, floats and widths
Stack windows into one column, float one above the row, or cycle a column between half, two thirds and full width.
It remembers your row
After a restart, windows come back in the same order and at the same widths.
Shortcuts you can change
Every shortcut can be rebound in Settings, along with spacing, swipe speed and scroll direction.

Shortcuts

Hold ⌃⌥ and press the key. All of these can be changed.

  • ← →Move focus along the row
  • ⇧← ⇧→Swap a window with its neighbor
  • RCycle width: half, two thirds, full
  • FFill the screen
  • CCenter the window
  • [ ]Stack into the column on the left or right
  • ⇧[ ⇧]Pop out of a stack
  • TFloat or unfloat
  • PPin to the right edge, then the left, then off
  • NSend to the other display
  • SpaceWindow picker
  • ORow overview

Install

  1. Download and open it

    Unzip StripTile.zip and move StripTile to Applications. It lives in the menu bar and has no Dock icon.

  2. Allow it past Gatekeeper

    The build is not notarized, so macOS will refuse the first launch. Control-click the app, choose Open, then confirm. Or open System Settings, Privacy & Security, and choose Open Anyway.

  3. Grant Accessibility

    StripTile needs this to read and move other apps' windows. Turn it on under System Settings, Privacy & Security, Accessibility. The app starts working as soon as you do.

  4. Free up four-finger swipes

    Open System Settings, Trackpad, More Gestures, and turn off the four-finger swipe between full-screen apps. Otherwise macOS and StripTile both react. The menu bar icon tells you when this is still on.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. It is MIT licensed, with no account, no payment and no tracking. The app contains no networking code at all.

Why is it not notarized?

Notarization needs a paid Apple Developer membership, and this is a free project. The release is signed ad hoc. The source is public, and ./bundle.sh builds the same app on your own Mac.

What does the Accessibility permission do?

It lets StripTile read where your windows are and move them. That is the only thing it is used for.

Does the swipe use anything private?

Yes. Reading raw trackpad touches goes through Apple's private MultitouchSupport framework, and Apple can change it in any macOS update. If that happens the swipe may stop working. Every keyboard shortcut will keep working.

Can I still use Mission Control and App Exposé?

StripTile ignores swipes that start out vertical, so four fingers up and down can stay with macOS. Turn them on in Trackpad settings. This was tuned on one trackpad, so tell us if it misfires on yours.

Where do the ideas come from?

Scrolling window managers such as niri, PaperWM and Paneru. StripTile is a separate app written in Swift and shares no code with them.

Does it work on Intel Macs?

The download is built for Apple silicon. On an Intel Mac, build it from source.
